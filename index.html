Pronom réfléchi

自反代词 are used with pronominal verbs such as se laver, se lever, and s’appeler. They stay before the verb: Je me lave. In negation, they stay with the verb: Je ne me lave pas.

Mini pattern notes

Je becomes j’ before vowel sounds: j’ai, j’habite.

Tu is informal “you.” Vous is formal singular or plural “you.”

On often means everyday spoken “we,” but it still takes singular verb forms: on est, on a.

Ils is “they” for a masculine or mixed group. Elles is “they” for an all-female group.

Verb index

Irregular Verbs

These high-frequency verbs do not follow the regular -er ending pattern, so it helps to learn each full present-tense set.

Aller — to go (irregular)

Aller changes a lot in the present tense, so it is best memorized as a core pattern.

Venir — to come (irregular)

Venir is another common irregular verb. Notice the stem changes: viens / vient, venons, venez, viennent.

Prendre — to take / to have (irregular)

Prendre is highly versatile. It is used for transport, food and drinks, time, photos, medicine, decisions, appointments, and expressions such as prendre soin de. Pronunciation changes noticeably: prends / prend use /pʁɑ̃/, while prenons uses /pʁə.nɔ̃/, prenez uses /pʁə.ne/, and prennent uses /pʁɛn/.

Faire — to do / to make (irregular)

Faire is very common and irregular. Compare fais / fait /fɛ/, faisons /fə.zɔ̃/, faites /fɛt/, and font /fɔ̃/.

Common faire expressions

These expressions are best learned as phrases. They do not need every pronoun form.

Voir — to see (irregular)

Voir changes to voyons and voyez in the nous/vous forms, while vois / voit / voient are all pronounced like vwa.

Écrire — to write (irregular)

Écrire changes from écris / écrit to écrivons, écrivez, and écrivent.

Lire — to read (irregular)

Lire has short singular forms lis / lit, then lisons, lisez, and lisent.

Similar Irregular Verbs

Dormir, partir, sortir, servir, and sentir share a useful pattern: singular forms drop part of the stem, while plural forms bring it back.

Dormir — to sleep (irregular pattern)

Dormir: dors / dort, then dormons, dormez, dorment.

Partir — to leave (irregular pattern)

Partir has silent final consonants in the singular: pars / part sound like /paʁ/, but partent pronounces the t.

Sortir — to go out / leave (irregular pattern)

Sortir follows the same family pattern: sors / sort, then sortons, sortez, sortent.

Servir — to serve (irregular pattern)

Servir changes from sers / sert to servons, servez, and servent.

Sentir — to smell / feel (irregular pattern)

Sentir changes from sens / sent to sentons, sentez, and sentent.

Regular Verbs

These verbs mostly follow the regular -er present-tense pattern: -e, -es, -e, -ons, -ez, -ent. A few keep pronunciation stable with spelling changes, like nous commençons and nous voyageons.

Habiter — to live (regular -er)

Habiter follows the regular -er pattern. The je form becomes j’habite.

Trouver — to find / to think (regular -er)

Trouver follows the regular -er pattern and is useful for both “find” and “think/find something to be.” The reflexive form se trouver commonly means “to be located.”

Regarder — to watch / to look at (regular -er)

Regarder follows the regular -er pattern and is used for watching something or looking at something directly.

Passer — to pass / stop by / spend (regular -er)

Passer is flexible: use it for passing through a place, stopping by, taking an exam, or spending time.

Commencer — to begin / to start (regular -er with spelling change)

Commencer keeps the soft c sound in the nous form by changing c to ç: nous commençons.

Voyager — to travel (regular -er with spelling change)

Voyager keeps the soft g sound in the nous form by adding e: nous voyageons.

Travailler — to work (regular -er)

Travailler follows the regular -er pattern and is useful for work, study, and schedule sentences.

Déjeuner — to have lunch (regular -er)

Déjeuner follows the regular -er pattern. In France, it usually means “to have lunch.”

Rentrer — to return / go home (regular -er)

Rentrer follows the regular -er pattern. Use it for returning home, coming back from a place, or going back inside.

Demander — to ask (regular -er)

Demander can mean “ask for” something, and demander à quelqu’un de faire quelque chose means “ask someone to do something.”

Jouer — to play (regular -er)

Use jouer à / au / aux for sports and games, and jouer de / du / de la for instruments.

Nager — to swim (regular -er with spelling change)

Nager keeps the soft g sound in the nous form by adding e: nous nageons.

Tourner — to turn (regular -er)

Tourner follows the regular -er pattern. Use it for turning left or right, rotating an object, going around something, and filming a movie.

Coûter — to cost (regular -er)

Coûter follows the regular -er pattern. The accent stays in the written forms.

Le Verbe Pronominal

Pronominal verbs use a reflexive pronoun: me, te, se, nous, vous, se. In negation, the reflexive pronoun stays with the verb: Je ne me lave pas. Questions can use inversion or est-ce que: À quelle heure te lèves-tu ?

Se laver — to wash oneself

Se laver is useful for daily routines. With body parts, French often says se laver les mains. The base verb laver means “to wash” something or someone: Je lave la voiture.

Se lever — to get up

Se lever changes spelling in the singular and third-person plural: lève, but levons and levez. The base verb lever means “to lift / raise”: Je lève la main.

Se reposer — to rest

Se reposer follows the regular -er pattern after the reflexive pronoun. The base verb reposer can mean “to put back / set down” something: Je repose le livre sur la table.

S’appeler — to be called / be named

S’appeler is the correct spelling. Notice je m’appelle, nous nous appelons, and ils s’appellent. The base verb appeler means “to call” someone: J’appelle ma mère.

S’habiller — to get dressed

S’habiller elides before the mute h: je m’habille, tu t’habilles, il s’habille. The base verb habiller means “to dress” someone: J’habille mon enfant.

Passé composé quick notes

Most verbs Use avoir in the present tense + past participle: j’ai parlé, tu as fini.
Some movement verbs Use être: elle est allée, nous sommes partis. The past participle agrees with the subject.

17 verbs that usually use être

These verbs often come in pairs, so this list keeps the paired learning order instead of strict alphabetical order. Some verbs also use avoir with a direct object or a different meaning.

-er verbs → -é

For regular -er verbs, remove -er and add -é: parler → parlé.

-ir verbs → -i

Regular -ir verbs often change to -i: finir → fini, choisir → choisi.

Irregular past participles

These high-frequency verbs have past participles you mostly memorize: eu, été, vu, fait, pris, and more.

Pronominal verbs → être + past participle

Pronominal verbs use être in passé composé: je me suis lavé, elle s’est habillée.

French Adverbs & Modifiers

Quick Notes

bien Bien is the adverb: it often means “well,” “fine,” or a judgment like “that’s good.” It does not agree.
bon Bon is an adjective for “good,” especially quality, usefulness, taste, or character: un bon café, une bonne idée.
beau Beau is an adjective for “beautiful / nice-looking,” and appears in weather: Il fait beau.

Time duration: pour vs dans

Definite vs Indefinite Articles

Compare the with a / an / some in three aligned rows. L’ is omitted here to keep the beginner table aligned.

L’article partitif: du / de la / de l’ / des

Use partitive articles for an unspecified amount of something, especially food, drink, materials, and abstract nouns. English often says only the noun: Je bois du café. = “I drink coffee.”

À / De + definite articles

À can mean to / at / in; de can mean of / from. Both combine with some definite articles.

À + articles

À + le = au, à + les = aux. À la and à l’ stay separate.

De + articles

De + le = du, de + les = des. De la and de l’ stay separate.

Demonstrative determiners

French Question Words

Quick Pattern Notes

Start here, then jump into the focused tables below.

Informal speech French often places question words at the end: Tu fais quoi ?
Before nouns Quel changes form to match the noun: quel, quelle, quels, quelles.
With vowels Que becomes qu’ before a vowel sound: Qu’est-ce que c’est ?
Yes/no questions Est-ce que starts a yes/no question. N’est-ce pas works like “isn’t it?” or “right?”

Quel Deep Dive

Quel means “which / what” before a noun. It agrees with the noun: masculine/feminine and singular/plural.

Changing sentence order

Special case: quel + être

Use this pattern when quel asks for identity, choice, time, date, address, or a similar “what is / which is” answer.

Special expression: de quelle couleur

Use de quelle couleur to ask what color something is. It often works with être: De quelle couleur est ... ?

Day questions

Use quel jour for day-of-week questions, and simple yes/no questions for days like lundi, mardi, and so on.

Time questions

Use quelle heure to ask “what time is it?” and à quelle heure to ask when something happens.

Que / Quoi Deep Dive

For object questions meaning what, compare the spoken quoi ending, the fronted que form, and the everyday qu’est-ce que pattern.

Special case: c’est quoi / qu’est-ce que c’est

Use this fixed pattern when asking what is it / what is that.

Comment Deep Dive

Comment asks how something happens or is done. Compare casual spoken order, formal inversion, and the neutral comment est-ce que pattern.

Combien Deep Dive

Combien asks about an amount or price. Before a noun, use combien de + noun; de stays unchanged even before a plural noun.

Y a-t-il Deep Dive

Y a-t-il is the formal question form of il y a, meaning is there / are there. Compare it with casual intonation and the everyday est-ce qu’il y a pattern.

Quick Notes

Lowercase in French Days, months, and seasons are usually lowercase: lundi, janvier, hiver.
Months French commonly uses en for months: en janvier.
Seasons French usually uses en, but spring is very often au printemps.

An / année, jour / journée, soir / soirée

The short word often marks the countable point or unit. The longer -ée word often emphasizes the lived span, content, or quality of that time.
